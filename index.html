The College Survival Guide

Quick visual manuals for washing, drying, and preventing fabric damage on a student budget.

Wash, Dry & Iron Manual

Care Essentials

Essential mechanical and thermal safeguards for communal dorm washers and dryers.

  • • Sort by fabric weight: Heavy denim jeans, hoodies, and towels violently beat up lightweight t-shirts in the drum. Wash heavy garments separately to prevent friction damage.
  • • Keep heat low for synthetics: High dryer heat melts, warps, and weakens polyester, nylon, and elastane gym wear. Always dry synthetics on low heat or hang dry.
  • • Iron inside-out: Soleplates can create permanent shiny burn marks on dark cottons and synthetic blends. Always iron inside-out at the lowest required heat setting.

Detergent & Money Saving

Cost Efficiency

Lower your utility costs while protecting clothes from shrinking with smart laundry chemistry.

  • • Cold water saves real money: Around 90% of a washer's energy is spent heating water. Washing at 20°C - 30°C cuts electricity bills significantly and prevents clothes from shrinking.
  • • Avoid heavy dyes & perfumes: Read detergent labels carefully. Heavy fragrances and dyes coat fibers with waxy residue that traps sweat bacteria and stiffens towels.
  • • Measure your detergent: Modern formulas are concentrated. Pouring past line 2 on the cap leaves a sticky film that actually attracts more dirt.

Fabric Purchasing Red Flags

Smart Buying

How to inspect care tags before buying so you don't buy garments destined to fail in dorm washers.

  • • Beware cheap polyesters: Low-grade fast-fashion synthetics trap body odor permanently (bacteria bonds to polyester) and pill after two heavy cycles.
  • • Invest in durable natural staples: 100% combed cotton, linen blends, and modal survive frequent heavy washing cycles without thinning or losing structure.
  • • Check tag care requirements: If a basic everyday college shirt has a "Dry Clean Only" circle symbol, skip it. Cleaning costs will exceed the garment price in weeks.

The Reckless Washing Graveyard

Avoid Disasters

Disastrous laundry habits that cause permanent, irreversible clothing destruction.

  • • The hot wool shrink catastrophe: Thermal shock from warm water and tumble dryers causes wool scales to interlock tightly, irreversibly shrinking your favorite knit into doll clothing.
  • • Washer drum overloading: Jamming a week of clothes into a single load prevents water movement, causing violent friction tears, broken zippers, and stretched collars.
  • • Detergent residue buildup: Excess detergent doesn't wash cleaner; it leaves a sticky residue buildup that traps mildew odor and irritates your skin.
